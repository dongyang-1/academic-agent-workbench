# Research workflow

Use this reference for literature inventory, thematic synthesis, formal review writing, and citation verification.

## 1. Collection inventory

Create one record per parent bibliographic item. Do not count PDF attachments as separate studies.

Minimum fields:

|Field|Requirement|
|---|---|
|Author|Match Zotero metadata and the original document|
|Year|Use the publication year, not import date|
|Title|Preserve the source title|
|Publication|Journal, book, report, or working-paper source|
|Research object|Market, institution, firm, household, region, instrument, or policy|
|Core question|What the study actually asks|
|Mechanism|Only if stated or tested in the source|
|Data and sample|Period, unit, sample size, and source when available|
|Method|Model, experiment, identification strategy, network method, or qualitative design|
|Core findings|Use qualified wording consistent with the source|
|Relevance|Direct, mechanism-transfer, method-transfer, or background|
|Theme|Provisional thematic placement|
|Core literature|Yes, secondary, or no, with a reason|
|Read status|Full text, abstract, or metadata|
|Evidence location|PDF page, printed page, section, table, figure, or abstract|
|Zotero identity|Library ID and item key when available|

If only an abstract or metadata is available, explicitly leave unsupported fields unresolved.

## 2. Evidence card

For every item likely to be cited, record:

- bibliographic metadata;
- research question;
- proposed or tested mechanism;
- data/sample;
- method and identification strength;
- main result and its limitations;
- representative source passage or faithful paraphrase;
- precise location;
- relevance to the target paper;
- potential citation risks.

Separate what the paper **finds** from what the reviewer **infers**.

## 3. Thematic classification

Prefer categories that explain the user's research problem. Possible dimensions include:

- risk formation and pricing;
- institutional or policy mechanisms;
- financing channels and substitution;
- spillover, contagion, and networks;
- measurement and identification methods;
- heterogeneous effects and boundary conditions.

Do not force a preset taxonomy. Adjust categories to the actual collection and explain material changes.

## 4. Viewpoint–literature matrix

Each thematic section should contain viewpoint records with this structure:

```text
Viewpoint:
Supporting literature:
Representative evidence:
Relationship to adjacent viewpoints:
Differences in object/sample/variable/method/mechanism:
Confidence and limitations:
```

Then summarize the theme across five dimensions:

1. consensus;
2. disagreement;
3. research evolution;
4. method differences;
5. unresolved questions.

Do not use the matrix as a stack of article abstracts. Each viewpoint should normally be supported by more than one study when the collection permits.

## 5. Formal literature review

Use the paragraph logic:

```text
central claim
→ evidence from several studies
→ agreement, complementarity, disagreement, or extension
→ implication, boundary, or remaining issue
```

Useful relationship transitions include equivalents of:

- further;
- in contrast;
- from the perspective of risk contagion;
- on this basis;
- these findings are complementary rather than contradictory;
- the apparent difference arises from the research object or channel.

Avoid:

- consecutive “Author (year) finds…” sentences;
- overstating association as causation;
- using a method-transfer paper as direct evidence for a different market;
- inserting uncited generalizations merely to make the narrative complete.

## 6. Research review and contribution

Derive gaps only after synthesis. Evaluate whether the collection supports gaps in:

- research object;
- outcome variable;
- cross-unit spillover or network perspective;
- policy identification;
- mechanism testing;
- multi-layer network design;
- heterogeneity;
- sample period or institutional context.

State gaps neutrally. A useful contribution connects an established literature boundary to a concrete research design rather than claiming that prior work is absent or defective.

## 7. Citation audit

Audit every citation occurrence, not merely every cited paper.

Recommended fields:

- sequence number;
- section and paragraph;
- displayed citation;
- author and year;
- title and publication;
- exact prose supported;
- viewpoint supported;
- source basis and page;
- read status;
- verification result;
- risk level;
- human-review flag;
- Zotero item key and library ID;
- notes.

Suggested verification results:

- verified;
- substantially consistent;
- pending verification;
- possibly overstated;
- inconsistent with source.

Suggested risk levels:

- **Low** — direct, qualified statement supported by located full-text evidence.
- **Medium** — multi-source synthesis, mechanism inference, missing exact page, strong causal wording, limited conditions, or one source reused across distinct claims.
- **High** — metadata-only support, unresolved author/year, claim stronger than source, conflicting evidence, or an unmatched source.

## 8. Completion gates

Before formal delivery, confirm:

- collection count reconciles;
- every cited item exists in the scoped collection;
- every substantive claim has source support;
- authors and years match Zotero;
- citation and bibliography sets are identical unless explicitly justified;
- unresolved metadata is visible, not guessed;
- gap claims are scoped correctly;
- all deliverables reflect the same citation state.
